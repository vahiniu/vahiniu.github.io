TriageBridge

Weekend side project · a health app that actually triages you

A little health companion I built that does the one thing most health apps skip: it decides how worried you should be, and gets a real person looped in when it matters.

A walkthrough of TriageBridge

I kept running into the same gap: health apps are great at showing you numbers, but terrible at telling you what to do with them. So over a weekend I built TriageBridge, a patient-facing app where you tap where it hurts on a body map, describe what's going on, and it sorts the symptom into emergent, urgent, or routine instead of leaving you to guess (or spiral on Google at 2am).

The name is the whole idea: it's a bridge. When something looks serious, the case gets escalated and lands in a physician's consult queue, sorted by acuity, so the crushing-chest-pain case sits at the top and the sore knee waits its turn. I built both sides for the demo: the patient app and the clinician inbox, so you can watch a symptom go from "I don't feel right" to a triaged card on a doctor's screen.

Around the triage core I stuffed in the stuff that makes people actually open a health app: a home dashboard with a composite risk score and your vitals, medication reminders you can check off, and a goals tab where you tell it an outcome ("lose 5kg before the wedding") and it builds daily habits tuned to your own data, gamified with points and streaks, because that's what makes me stick with anything. My favorite part is the "what if" screen: drag a slider to shed a few kilos or drop your blood pressure and the real risk models (10-year cardiovascular risk, FINDRISC for diabetes, BMI) recompute live, so you can see what your habits are actually buying you. It's a modeled estimate, not a promise, but it's a lot more motivating than a static number.

It's a prototype, not a product (no clinical claims here), but it was a fun excuse to mash together the two things I think about most: rigorous medical logic and an interface a normal person would actually use.
