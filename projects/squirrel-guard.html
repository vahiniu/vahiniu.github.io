The Squirrel Guard

Side project · Raspberry Pi · on-device computer vision

A Raspberry Pi that watches our vegetable garden, works out whether the thing that just moved is actually a squirrel, and if it is, swings a little arm into a windchime. Harmless, chemical-free, and weirdly satisfying.

We lose vegetables to squirrels. Not occasionally, but pretty much every time something's ready to pick, they get to it first. We tried neem oil. We tried draping mesh over everything. We even tried growing just chillis. None of it worked. (Fun fact: a small portion of squirrels, like us humans, get addicted to spice.)

So I went looking for a real solution, and everything online was either ultrasonic or toxic. The problem is our neighbors have pets, and we didn't want to put out anything that might affect them. That ruled out most of what's sold for this. So I built my own instead — something safe, harmless to animals, and honestly kind of fun to have running.

Here's how it works. A Raspberry Pi with a camera watches the garden. When something moves, a small AI model sitting on the Pi checks whether it's actually a squirrel (not a bird, not a blowing leaf), and if it is, a servo swings a little 3D-printed arm that knocks a windchime to scare it off. That's it. No chemicals, no ultrasonic noise, nothing that bothers the neighbors' pets. Just a startling jingle aimed only at the squirrels.

Has it worked? In progress… ask me at the end of the season. It also photographs every animal that visits and logs them to a little web dashboard, which has turned into a surprisingly fun way to check who's been in the backyard.

The one catch: it's built to survive outdoors, but I don't have a power outlet anywhere near the garden, so I haven't figured out how to make it a permanent fixture yet. For now we run it off a power bank during the day (I added battery-saving options to the code so it lasts longer) and bring it in to recharge overnight. Squirrels are daytime raiders anyway, so it mostly works out. A proper always-on setup is still on the to-do list.
